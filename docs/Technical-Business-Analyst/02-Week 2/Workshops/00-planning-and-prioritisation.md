## Workshop

<LockedContent password="ownership">

### Communication

**Ask** trainees how they communicated with stakeholders in the previous week.

**Probe** further: why do we communicate with stakeholders?

- To get more information
- To validate choices
- To receive feedback
- To keep them updated

**Highlight** that an analyst without information or stakeholder buy-in cannot work effectively.

**Challenge** trainees to identify at least two ways they will interact with stakeholders this week.

### Scope

**Explain** that this week shifts from diagnosis to decisions: which capabilities go into Phase 1, and which do we exclude?

**Ask** trainees to define "scope": the boundaries of what the project will and will not cover.

**Discuss** how scope creep happens when boundaries aren't clear or defended.

**Return** to the [PVC case study](https://sigma-resources-public.s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/tba-datasets/pvc/Case+Study_+Pritzki+Veterinary+Clinic.pdf).

### Challenge: Idea Generation

**Challenge** trainees to come up with as many suggestions as possible to improve PVC's situation.

**Emphasise** that this is 'blue-sky' thinking time: focus on idea generation, not criticism.

**Grant** trainees ~5 minutes to come up with suggestions.

### What Phase 1 means

**Explain** that Phase 1 should capture the smallest coherent set of capabilities that produces visible business value.

**Highlight** useful tests for inclusion:

- Does it solve a top-ranked problem?
- Is it needed for the main customer journey?
- Is it foundational for later work?
- Can it be delivered without major legacy redesign?

### From opportunity list to product approach

**Explain** that strong analysts do not simply say, "These are the top five opportunities." They also say, "This is the smallest believable slice that delivers value."

**Demonstrate** the difference between:

> Weak scope: "Build a scheduling solution."
>
> Better scope: "Deliver a focused Phase 1 that supports central scheduling, self-serve emergency booking for customers, & remote calendar viewing for staff only. No payment/billing handling will be considered. 

**Stress** that scope quality improves when exclusions are named explicitly.

### Prioritisation

**Introduce** limitations: PVC have two weeks to make changes, and no budget for external developers/staff.

**Ask** trainees how we can effectively prioritise suggestions.

**Gather** suggestions:

- ROI
- Risk level
- Time-to-implement
- Stakeholder priorities
- Frameworks such as the [Eisenhower Matrix](https://asana.com/resources/eisenhower-matrix)

**Explain** that there is no one perfect prioritisation system; what matters is making *reasonable* and *defensible* choices.

**Ask** trainees to collectively prioritise suggestions into in-/out-of-scope.

**Question** trainees, looking for justifications/trade-offs. Be adversarial: demand in-depth explanations.

**Discuss** each answer using the lenses of value, complexity, risk, and traceability back to evidence.

**State** that being able to make a clear case for inclusion/exclusion is a key part of getting stakeholder buy-in.

### Challenge

**Challenge** trainees individually to write a one-paragraph Phase 1 concept statement that includes:

- the customer journeys included
- one major exclusion
- one operational safeguard
- one reason the scope is credible

**Grant** trainees 10 minutes.

**Gather** responses and ask the room whether each statement sounds like a deliverable first release rather than a vague ambition.

### Framing the week ahead

**Check** trainee understanding of the week's case study.

**Answer** any logistical questions; encourage trainees to contact stakeholders to discuss others.

**Highlight** that this week, we're moving from collecting evidence (discovery) to making precise proposals/recommendations.

**Stress** that the rest of the week is about proving this recommendation can survive both operational and executive scrutiny.
