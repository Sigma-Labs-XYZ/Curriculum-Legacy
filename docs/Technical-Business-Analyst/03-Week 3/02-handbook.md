## 2. Communication Etiquette and Memorandum Formatting Standards

To maintain the prestigious LexCorp brand, all internal and external communications must adhere strictly to the LexCorp Style Guide (last updated 2014). The following typographical rules are non-negotiable:

- **Font Face and Sizing:** All internal memorandums must be drafted in Garamond, size 11.5. Exceptions are made only for footnote text, which may be reduced to size 9.5. Arial and Calibri are strictly forbidden and their use will result in an immediate dressing-down by your Practice Group Leader. Times New Roman is acceptable only for external correspondence directed to the federal courts, per local rules.
- **Spacing and Margins:** Documents must feature exactly 1.15 line spacing. Margins must be 1.25 inches on the left and right, and 1.0 inch on the top and bottom.
- **The Oxford Comma:** LexCorp is a firm that believes in clarity, precision, and the Oxford comma. Failure to use the serial comma in a list of three or more items is considered a drafting error and will be noted in your file.
- **Email Salutations:** When emailing a Partner, associates must use "Dear Mr./Ms. [Last Name]," unless explicitly invited to use a first name. "Hi" or "Hey" are fireable offenses if directed to a Senior Partner. When CC'ing multiple people, they must be listed in strict order of firm seniority.

If a physical letter must be drafted, the firm's watermarked stationery (located only in the 5th-floor secured supply closet) must be used. Associates must sign in blue ink only; black ink implies a photocopy, and red ink is reserved exclusively for the Managing Partner's annotations.

## 3. Legal Operations: Contract & Document Review

Our legal operations team handles the intake, review, and summarization of thousands of client documents weekly.

### 3.1 Legacy Contract Clause Extraction (Force Majeure)

When a corporate client requires an audit of their legacy contracts (e.g., assessing liability during a global supply chain disruption), the following steps must be taken:

1. **Document Assignment:** The Senior Partner emails a zip file containing 50-200 PDF contracts to the paralegal pool.
2. **Review Process:** The assigned paralegal must open each PDF, read through the document to locate the "Force Majeure" or "Liability Limitation" clauses.
3. **Data Extraction:** The paralegal copies the exact text of the clause and pastes it into the Master Client Audit Excel Spreadsheet.
4. **Risk Assessment:** The paralegal must write a 1-2 sentence summary of the clause and manually assign a risk score from 1-10 based on the strictness of the liability limits.
5. **Submission:** The Excel sheet is emailed back to the Senior Partner for client presentation.

### 3.2 NDA Triage and Approval

Non-Disclosure Agreements (NDAs) are the gateway to new business, but they must be scrutinized to protect our clients.

- **Intake:** All client NDAs are sent to the shared inbox: `nda-review@lexcorp.com`.
- **Initial Read:** A junior associate monitors this inbox, downloads the PDF, and reads it line-by-line comparing it against our "Standard Acceptable Terms" physical binder.
- **Routing:**
  - If the NDA matches standard terms (e.g., standard jurisdiction, standard term length), the associate prints it, physically walks it to the Partner's desk for signature, scans it, and emails it back to the client.
  - If the NDA has deviations (e.g., perpetual term length), the associate must draft an email highlighting the specific issue and send it to the Partner for negotiation.

## 6. Risk & Compliance Management

Before any legal work begins, we must ensure strict ethical and regulatory compliance.

### 6.1 Conflict of Interest Clearing

We cannot represent a client if we are already representing their adversary. The Risk Team must clear all new clients within 3-5 business days.

1. A Partner submits a "New Client Intake Form" via an internal web portal.
2. The Risk Analyst logs into external databases (such as Dun & Bradstreet) to manually search for the prospective client's parent companies, subsidiaries, and major shareholders.
3. The Analyst then logs into LexCorp's legacy on-premise SQL database and runs manual text searches for the prospective client's name and all identified subsidiaries against our active client list.
4. If a potential match is found, the Analyst compiles a Word document detailing the overlapping matters and emails it to the Head of Risk for manual review and approval/rejection.

### 6.2 Client Hospitality and Gift Register

LexCorp is required by the SRA Code of Conduct to maintain a register of all hospitality given to and received from clients, referral sources, and third parties where the value may reasonably give rise to a perception of improper influence. This register is a regulatory obligation, not a suggestion.

The register is maintained in physical form only, held in the office of the Managing Partner's Executive Assistant. A digital version does not exist and will not be created.

- Any gift received from a client or third party with an estimated value exceeding £50 must be declared to your Practice Group Leader within 48 hours of receipt. The Practice Group Leader will determine whether the gift may be retained, declined, or donated to the firm's designated charity.
- Any hospitality provided to a client (meals, events, entertainment) with a value exceeding £100 per person must be logged in the register within five working days. The entry must include: the date, the client name, the nature of the hospitality, the approximate value per head, and the Partner sign-off.
- The register is reviewed quarterly by the Chief Risk & Compliance Officer.

Associates are reminded that the hospitality register exists precisely because it is meant to be uncomfortable to complete. If you are unsure whether something requires logging, the answer is: log it. If you are unsure who to ask, do not ask your direct line manager; contact the Managing Partner's office.

The firm does not publicise the contents of the hospitality register internally. Associates who make enquiries about entries other than their own will be directed to Risk & Compliance.

## 9. Finance and Accounts Payable

Accurate timekeeping and expense tracking are essential for firm profitability and client trust.

Our corporate clients have strict "Outside Counsel Guidelines" detailing what they will and will not pay for.

- **Submission:** At the end of every month, lawyers submit their time narratives into the billing system.

LexCorp receives hundreds of invoices weekly from expert witnesses, court reporters, and external software vendors.

1. Invoices arrive as PDFs to `accounts@lexcorp.com`.

4. The clerk manually keys the Vendor Name, Total Amount, and Matter Number into the firm's ERP system.
5. An email is generated and sent to the Lead Partner asking for payment approval.

## 10. The 1998 Restructuring and Corporate Real Estate Strategy

To fully understand LexCorp's current operational footprint, one must look back to the Great Restructuring of 1998. Driven by the merger with Hughes & Sterling, the firm was forced to consolidate its real estate holdings. The decision was made to abandon the East Wing of the downtown tower and consolidate entirely into the West Wing. The SATF determined that partners would retain corner offices, but associates would move to an open-plan pod configuration separated by frosted glass partitions. These partitions were specifically chosen to reduce glare from the CRT monitors prevalent at the time, though they are largely decorative today. In 2005, the HVAC system was overhauled to accommodate the increased heat output from the early server racks installed in the basement.

Today, our real estate strategy relies heavily on the "Desk-Booker" intranet portal. Associates who plan to be in the office on Tuesdays and Thursdays must log into the portal 48 hours in advance to reserve a desk in the "Quiet Zone." Failure to book a desk will result in the associate being relegated to the "Collaboration Hub," which is adjacent to the notoriously loud 3rd-floor breakout space.

## 12. Business Development & Advisory

Our partners must be positioned as thought leaders and trusted advisors.

### 12.1 Regulatory Client Alerts

Partners must proactively inform clients when new relevant laws or regulations are passed.

- **Monitoring:** Every morning from 8:00 AM to 9:30 AM, designated paralegals manually check the websites of the SEC, Financial Conduct Authority, and other regulatory bodies.
- **Drafting:** If a new ruling is posted, the paralegal reads the document (often 10-50 pages), writes a 3-bullet-point summary, and determines which legal sector it impacts.
- **Distribution:** The summary is emailed to the relevant Practice Group Partners so they can draft a formal client alert.

### 12.2 RFP Pitch Experience Compilation

When pitching for multi-million dollar corporate retainers, the Business Development (BD) team must prove LexCorp has handled similar matters before.

1. The BD Manager receives a Request for Proposal (RFP) for a niche area (e.g., "offshore drilling litigation").
2. The BD Manager sends an "ALL PARTNERS" blast email asking: _"Have we ever handled an offshore drilling dispute? Please reply with details."_
3. The BD team waits 48-72 hours for partners to check their own records and reply.
4. The BD team manually compiles the fragmented email replies into a "Relevant Experience" slide for the pitch deck.

### 12.3 Legal Directory and Rankings Submissions (Chambers & Partners, The Legal 500)

Each year, LexCorp submits practice area entries to Chambers & Partners and The Legal 500, the two primary legal directory publishers used by corporate clients to benchmark and select law firms. Strong rankings are commercially significant. A drop in a practice area's ranking has historically correlated with reduced panel instructions from fee-sensitive clients.

The submission process is coordinated by the Business Development team on a fixed annual timetable issued each January. Practice Group Leaders are responsible for drafting narrative content for their teams. Submissions typically include:

- A practice area narrative (500-800 words) summarising the team's capabilities, recent significant matters, and key personnel
- A list of notable matters from the preceding 12 months, with client permission obtained for each reference
- Referee nominations: clients willing to be contacted by directory researchers for confidential peer review

Associates may be asked to provide matter summaries or draft narrative sections for their Practice Group Leader's review. Drafts must be factually accurate, cleared for client confidentiality, and submitted within the deadline set by the BD team. Late submissions are not accepted by the publishers. A missed deadline means no ranking for that practice area that year.

The BD team does not, under any circumstances, fabricate or embellish matter descriptions. Associates who are asked to "round up" the scope or significance of a matter by a senior colleague are advised to raise this with the BD Director, not to comply silently.

### 12.4 Client Christmas Card List

The firm dispatches personalised Christmas cards to approximately 400 client contacts annually. This is a deliberate relationship-maintenance activity and is taken seriously at Partner level. The card list is curated and maintained by the Managing Partner's Executive Assistant and reflects the firm's current view of its active client relationships.

**Additions to the list** must be proposed by a Partner and approved by the relevant Practice Group Leader. The proposing Partner must confirm the contact's current role, correct postal address, and the nature of the LexCorp relationship. Cards are addressed by name and signed personally by the relationship Partner.

**Removals from the list** require a written note to the Managing Partner's Executive Assistant explaining the reason: client departure, matter closure, or what is internally referred to as a "relationship cooling." The note is retained on file. If a matter has concluded but the relationship remains active, the contact stays on the list until a Partner confirms otherwise.

The list is maintained in physical binder form only. No digital version exists, by deliberate policy, as the list is considered a sensitive document reflecting the state of the firm's commercial relationships. Associates should not request access to the list. If a client contact requires a card outside the standard annual cycle, for example a congratulatory note on a significant transaction close, this should be arranged separately with the Managing Partner's Executive Assistant.

Cards are dispatched no later than the 10th of December. The firm's embossed card stock is used, not standard office supplies. Do not use standard card stock for client correspondence of any kind.

Do not leave documents unattended in the fax tray.

## 16. Fair Pay Policy and Compensation Framework

LexCorp Partners is committed to fair and equitable compensation for all staff, regardless of role level or practice area. Our approach to salary determination is rooted in evidence-based market benchmarking and a commitment to equal treatment.

### 16.1 Salary Band Structure

LexCorp establishes salary bands for all operational staff roles (paralegals, junior associates, support staff, and administrative positions) based on published national salary survey data for equivalent roles in the professional services sector. These surveys are commissioned annually from recognized compensation research firms such as Payscale and Robert Half.

For each role, LexCorp establishes a **National Average Salary Reference Point** derived from the most recent market survey data. Individual compensation is then positioned within a band of **+/- 15% of this reference point**, depending on the employee's seniority, years of experience in the role, and demonstrated performance level.

**Band Positioning Examples:**
- An employee in their first 2 years in a paralegal role would typically be positioned at the lower end of the band (National Average - 12% to - 8%).
- An employee with 5-8 years' experience and strong performance evaluations would be positioned mid-band (National Average - 3% to + 3%).
- A senior paralegal with 10+ years' experience, special expertise, or critical function responsibilities would be positioned toward the upper end (National Average + 8% to + 15%).

### 16.2 Annual Band Review and Adjustment

The salary bands themselves are reviewed and adjusted annually, typically in January, to reflect updated market data from the compensation research firms. When market data indicates that the National Average Reference Point for a role has increased, the entire band is adjusted upward to maintain fair market positioning. This means all employees in that role category see their salary bands shift, regardless of individual performance.

**Individual Salary Review Process:**
- All employees receive an annual salary review during the January performance review cycle.
- The HR Compensation Coordinator will confirm the applicable role's salary band for the current year.
- If an employee's current salary falls outside the updated band boundaries, their salary will be adjusted to bring them into band (minimum: band floor; maximum: band ceiling).
- Individual performance, tenure, and experience are considered when determining the specific adjustment amount within the band.

### 16.3 Role Progression and Salary Advancement

Advancement within a salary band occurs through:
- **Annual Performance Review Consideration:** Employees with "Meets Expectations" or higher performance ratings may receive an annual salary increase of 2-4%, subject to the requirement that their new salary does not exceed their role's band ceiling.
- **Role Reclassification:** Employees whose responsibilities materially expand or who move into a different role category will be evaluated for placement in the new role's salary band, effective on the transition date.
- **Promotion:** When an employee is promoted to a higher-level role, they will be positioned within that role's new salary band. The positioning will be no lower than 1% above their previous salary and no higher than the band ceiling for the new role.

### 16.4 Exceptions and Appeals

Compensation decisions are governed by this policy and are binding. Appeals regarding salary band placement or annual review outcomes should be directed to the HR Director (not the HR Compensation Coordinator) in writing within 10 working days of notification. Appeals will be reviewed by the HR Director and the applicable Practice Group Leader. The outcome of the appeal process is final.
