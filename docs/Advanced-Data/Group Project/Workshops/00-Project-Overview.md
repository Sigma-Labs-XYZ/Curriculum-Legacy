### 1. Meet Your Team (5 mins)

You should already know who you'll be working with but if not, teams will be assigned now.

These are the same roles as last week. Assign yourselves to a role to assume above the role of [Engineer/Analyst](../Roles/engineer.md)

Spend some time reviewing the brief, planning out what your specific contribution as your role

## Workshop

**Prompt** trainees to reflect on the previous week's project

**Document** this in excalidraw

> - What did we learn last week?
> - What worked well last week?
> - What will we do differently this week?
> - What won't we do this week?

**Explain** the structure of these two weeks

> This project is similar to last week where you will be building a larger project. The structure remains the same, except now you have two weeks to complete the brief

**Provide** trainees with the general deadlines for:

- An ERD (by the end of Tuesday of the first week)
- An Architecture Diagram (by the end of Wednesday of the first week)
- A draft of the presentation and tech pack (by the end of Friday of the first week)

**Instruct** trainees that, like last week, the better their plan is, the more time they will have to create better features

### Assessment

**Show** trainees the [assessment criteria](../03-assessment.md#assessment-criteria)

- Code Quality and Documentation
- Pull Requests
- Tests
- Project Board
- Presentation
- Completeness (Is it a complex project and does it work?)

**Show** an example of a set of Pull Requests completed to a high standard

> https://github.com/AhmetCengiz03/football-project/pulls

### Presentation

**Provide** more information around the presentation

- These will be in the **afternoon** on the **last day** of the course, beginning around 1:30-2:30
- The presentations must be **5 minutes** long
- **Every member** of the group should be involved in delivering the presentation
- You will have a few **practice sessions** with PHP / Tech in the second week for feedback, but this does not mean you shouldn't practise in the first week
- **Start writing sooner**. Presentations that are written at the last minute _never_ look good

**Ask** trainees what a good presentation is

> - It gives the correct amount of detail (not too much, not too little)
> - It focuses on the business problem and how their solution solves the problem
> - It is data-driven, and focuses on the user journey
> - It is **well formatted** with a **uniting theme** other than a white background with black text and no images

**Outline** to trainees what the presentation should and should not contain:

> Do include
>
> - Target user(s)
> - User stories
> - A business problem
> - A solution to the problem
> - A clear colour scheme and theme
> - Images
> - A live demonstration of the dashboard
> - Architecture diagram
>
> Don't include
>
> - Code snippets
> - Specific functions
> - Any queries
> - Black text on a white background
> - Huge blocks of text
> - Over the top animations and transitions

**Remind** trainees who will be watching the final presentations

- Coaches
- Rich
- Rory
- Sales
- Any other member of the SL team

**Highlight** that many of the people in the presentation will have _no context_ of the project at all

**Tell** trainees that there will also be a demo-day, where they will be presenting to _real_ clients

**Explain** that they should be thinking about the presentation as soon as possible, and practice the presentation often. Many groups have lost marks because they hadn't rehearsed until the last few days

**Highlight** non-negotiables to the presentations

> Your presentations MUST have the following:
>
> - Above all else you are Data Consultants, and each of you have taken on an additional role
> - You are using impressive technology, tell us about: Terraform, Python, RDS, etc
> - Data protection strategies, or a basic understanding of GDPR

### Tech Pack

**Highlight** while the presentation is for explaining the **what** and the **why**, the tech pack is for explaining **how**

**Go** to the assessment page and go through the required contents of the tech pack

**Explain** the tech pack should be an appendix to the presentation, and therefore should be in the same style

**Highlight** the tech pack is to be read through before the presentation to get an understanding of the key components of your project

### General guidance

**Remind** trainees of the software development lifecycle and agile methodologies

> The goal here is to create a minimal viable product first, then iterate over it

**Advise** trainees that they should still be planning out their work in small, manageable tasks, with tickets and (well-commented upon) pull requests.

**Suggest** heavily that the first thing that trainees should be doing is meeting to go over their plans:

1. Booking in their daily meetings
2. Setting up the repo (and inviting the coaches)
3. Setting up their project board (and inviting the coaches)
4. Writing tickets for the project (and assigning the 'do now's)
5. Aligning on code quality / styling
6. Setting expectations for how they will be working

**Explain** that the groups that do the best are ones that focus on the simplest, most effective solution, then add complexity afterwards.

**Summarise** the general guidance for the week:

- Communicate with each other, frequently
- Take ownership of your tasks
- Ask for help sooner rather than later
- Simple is better than overly complex
- Get an MVP first, then expand
- Get the presentation done sooner rather than later
